SDLC SELECTION RULES
Source: NITK Software Engineering Lecture Slides

IMPORTANT:
These rules are derived primarily from the NITK SDLC selection slides.
General software engineering knowledge may supplement these rules when relevant,
but the lecture-slide rules should be given priority when they directly apply.


========================
1. WATERFALL MODEL
========================

Use Waterfall when:

- Requirements are very well documented.
- Requirements are clear and fixed.
- Product definition is stable.
- Technology is understood and not dynamic.
- Requirements are not ambiguous.
- The project is relatively short.
- The project is small or well understood.

Waterfall is less suitable when:

- Requirements change moderately or frequently.
- The project is complex or long-running.
- There is high uncertainty.
- Significant scope changes are expected.


========================
2. V-SHAPED MODEL
========================

Use V-Shaped when:

- Requirements are well defined.
- Requirements are clearly documented.
- Requirements are stable/fixed.
- Product definition is stable.
- Technology is understood.
- Requirements are not ambiguous.
- Strong verification and validation are required.
- Testing needs to be planned early.
- Each development phase should have corresponding testing.

V-Shaped is less suitable when:

- Requirements change dynamically.
- Iteration is required.
- Continuous changes are expected.
- Significant risk analysis is required.


========================
3. PROTOTYPE MODEL
========================

Use Prototype when:

- Requirements are unclear or need clarification.
- The desired system requires substantial end-user interaction.
- Continuous user feedback is important.
- The system has significant user-interface/user-interaction requirements.
- Demonstrating functionality will help identify requirements.
- A proof of concept may be required.

Benefits relevant to selection:

- Helps users understand the proposed system.
- Helps identify missing or confusing functionality.
- Provides early feedback.
- Allows requirements to be refined.


========================
4. RAD MODEL
========================

Use RAD when:

- Requirements may change.
- User involvement is high.
- The system can be divided into modules.
- Rapid development is required.
- Short iterations are appropriate.
- Early integration and customer feedback are important.
- The project can be completed in approximately 2–3 months.
- Skilled developers/designers are available.
- Adequate resources and budget are available.


========================
5. ITERATIVE / INCREMENTAL MODEL
========================

Use Iterative/Incremental when:

- Major requirements are known.
- Details of requirements may evolve.
- Early working functionality is required.
- Requirements may change.
- Functionality can be developed incrementally.
- Risk analysis is useful.
- New technology is involved.
- High-risk features can benefit from early development and evaluation.
- The project benefits from periodic working results.


========================
6. SPIRAL MODEL
========================

Use Spiral when:

- Risk evaluation and management are important.
- The project has medium or high risk.
- Requirements may change.
- Requirements are initially unclear.
- Requirements are complex and need clarification.
- Extensive prototyping may be useful.
- Long-term commitment is expected.
- Significant changes are expected.
- Early identification of risky parts is important.

Spiral combines iterative development/prototyping with
explicit risk analysis and management.


========================
7. AGILE MODEL
========================

Use Agile when:

- Requirements change frequently.
- Continuous stakeholder/customer involvement is important.
- Frequent feedback is required.
- Frequent delivery of working functionality is useful.
- Development needs to be iterative and adaptive.
- The project needs to respond quickly to changing requirements.


========================
PROJECT CHARACTERISTICS TO ANALYZE
========================

The SDLC decision should consider:

1. Requirement uncertainty
2. Requirement stability
3. Requirement change frequency
4. Functional needs
5. Project team experience
6. Technology experience
7. Resource availability
8. User community
9. User involvement
10. Project type
11. Project complexity
12. Cost/budget
13. Cost of future upgrades
14. Requirement change pattern
15. Reuse
16. Project longevity
17. Product technology
18. Project risk
19. Need for prototype
20. Need for proof of concept
21. Need for verification and validation


========================
GENERAL SOFTWARE ENGINEERING
FACTORS
========================

The system may additionally consider factors particularly
relevant to financial software:

- Regulatory criticality
- Security risk
- Privacy sensitivity
- Auditability
- Compliance requirements
- Consequences of failure
- Legacy-system dependencies
- Integration complexity
- Frequency of regulatory changes
- Deployment/release requirements

These factors supplement the NITK lecture rules.
They must not override a directly applicable lecture rule without
an explicit explanation.


========================
DECISION PRINCIPLE
========================

The system must NOT select an SDLC merely because the project
belongs to a particular domain.

Instead:

Project Requirements
        ↓
Extract Project Characteristics
        ↓
Apply NITK SDLC Selection Rules
        ↓
Consider Additional Software Engineering Factors
        ↓
Identify Applicable SDLC Models
        ↓
Compare Evidence
        ↓
Recommend SDLC
        ↓
Explain Recommendation
        ↓
Human Approval


The system should also allow a hybrid recommendation when
the project characteristics genuinely support characteristics
of multiple SDLC models.

The final recommendation must explain:

- Which project characteristics were identified.
- Which NITK rules were triggered.
- Which additional SE factors were relevant.
- Which SDLC models were considered.
- Why the selected model fits the identified characteristics.
- Why other applicable models were not selected.
- What evidence supports the recommendation.
